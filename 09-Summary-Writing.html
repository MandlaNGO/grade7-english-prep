Module 9: Summary Writing

Reading & Viewing - writing main ideas in your own words, in paragraph form.

Grade 7 · English Home Language (HL) · Term 4 exam revision · 2 x 60 minutes

By the end of this module you can:

  • Explain what a summary is and what it is not.
  • Use the seven-step summary method on a non-fiction passage.
  • Write a summary in paragraph form, in my own words, within the word limit.
  • Link points with connectives without copying whole sentences.
  • Mark a summary against a mini checklist.

1. Learner Notes

What is a summary?

A summary is the main ideas of a passage, in your own words, in logical order, in paragraph form, within a word limit.

  • Keep: only the main ideas.
  • Leave out: examples, repetition, descriptions, dialogue and your own opinions.

The seven steps

  1. Read the passage twice - once for meaning, once for structure.
  2. Find the topic sentence of each paragraph (usually first or last sentence).
  3. Strike out examples, numbers, repetition and extra detail.
  4. Underline the main points - usually one per paragraph.
  5. Paraphrase each point in your own words.
  6. Join the points with connectives to make a flowing paragraph.
  7. Count your words and edit until you are within the limit.

Do and don't

DoDon't
Use your own wordsCopy whole sentences from the passage
Write one flowing paragraphWrite a list of bullet points
Keep the order of the passageJump between ideas
Count your wordsGo over the word limit
Use connectives (firstly, then, as a result)Add your own opinion or new ideas

Connective bank

firstly, secondly, next, then, in addition, also, as a result, because, finally

Word limit discipline

A 50-60 word limit usually holds 4-5 joined points. If you are over, cut adjectives and repeated ideas - never cut main points.

2. Interactive Practice

3. Worked Example

Mini passage

The school recycling club started with only three members. Today it has thirty learners who collect paper, tins and plastic every Friday. The club also runs a vegetable garden using compost made from fruit peels. Last term the club raised R2 400 by selling seedlings.

Model summary (25 words)

The recycling club grew from three to thirty members, collects recycling weekly, runs a compost-fed vegetable garden and raised R2 400 selling seedlings.

Why it works

  • Every paragraph gave one point (growth, recycling collection, garden, money raised).
  • Small details ('every Friday', 'fruit peels') were cut.
  • Words changed: 'started with only three' became 'grew from three'.
  • Points joined with a flow, not a list.

4. Worksheet

Passage: Be Safe Online

Being safe online starts with strong habits. First, never share your full name, address, phone number or school with strangers online. Secondly, use a password that mixes letters, numbers and symbols, and keep it secret - even from friends. Thirdly, think before you click: links in strange messages can steal your information or damage your device. Remember that people are not always who they say they are, so do not agree to meet anyone you met online. If something makes you feel scared or uncomfortable, close the app and tell a trusted adult immediately. Finally, be kind: what you post can hurt others and can stay online forever. Treat others online the way you want to be treated.

Task (10 marks)

Write a summary of the passage in 50-60 words, in your own words, in ONE paragraph. (10)

Self-check before you check the memo

  • Did I use my own words?
  • Did I keep only main ideas (no examples)?
  • Is it one paragraph with connectives?
  • Is it between 50 and 60 words?

To stay safe online, keep personal information private and use secret, strong passwords. Think before clicking unknown links, and remember that people online may not be honest. Never meet online strangers; if you feel uncomfortable, tell a trusted adult. Always be kind, because posts can hurt and last forever.

Mark allocation

  • Content points: any 7 correct main points = 7 marks.
  • Own words: 1 mark.
  • Within 50-60 words: 1 mark.
  • Logical paragraph with connectives: 1 mark.
  • Total: 10.

6. Exam-style Question & Memo

Paper 2, Q3 style - Summary (10 marks, about 15 minutes)

Read the passage and summarise it in your own words in 50-60 words, in ONE paragraph.

Passage: Recycling at School

Many schools produce far too much waste. Paper, cardboard, tins and plastic bottles fill the bins every single day. A simple way to fix this is to place labelled bins in every classroom. Learners then sort their rubbish before they throw it away. The school can sell the recyclable material to local recycling companies, which brings in extra money for sport equipment. Food waste can also be used: fruit peels and vegetable scraps make excellent compost for the school garden. Recycling teaches responsibility, and it keeps the school grounds clean and healthy. Most importantly, learners who recycle at school usually carry the same habit into their homes and communities.

Show memo (click to reveal)

Main points

  • Schools produce too much waste.
  • Label bins in every classroom so learners sort rubbish.
  • Recyclable material can be sold for extra money.
  • Food waste becomes compost for the school garden.
  • Recycling teaches responsibility and keeps the school clean.
  • Learners take the habit home to their communities.

Model summary (55 words)

Schools can reduce their waste by putting labelled bins in classrooms so learners sort rubbish. The recyclable material can be sold to earn money for the school, and food waste can be turned into compost for the garden. Recycling teaches responsibility, keeps the school clean and spreads to learners' homes and communities.

Marks: content 7 (one per point, max 7) - own words 1 - word limit 1 - paragraph flow 1 = 10.

7. Remember this!

One-glance recap - if a line feels fuzzy, scroll back to the notes.

  • Main ideas only - drop examples, repeated points and small details.
  • Use your own words and keep close to the word limit; count words at the end.
  • Paragraph form (unless told otherwise) - one point flowing into the next.
  • No opinions, no copied sentences.

Lesson plan, differentiation, exit ticket and homework for this module.

Lesson Plan

Learning objectives

  • Explain what a summary is and what it is not.
  • Use the seven-step summary method on a non-fiction passage.
  • Write a summary in paragraph form, in my own words, within the word limit.
  • Link points with connectives without copying whole sentences.
  • Mark a summary against a mini checklist.

Prior knowledge

Learners can find the main idea of a paragraph and paraphrase a sentence.

Resources

Learner notes, practice passages and model summaries; highlighters.

Lesson flow (2 x 60 minutes)

  1. Session 1: What a summary is + the seven steps, modelled on a mini passage.
  2. Session 1: Guided practice - class shortens a paragraph together.
  3. Session 2: 'Be Safe Online' worksheet under time (20 minutes), then peer marking with the checklist.
  4. Session 2: Exam-style passage 'Recycling at School' as a timed exit task.

Success criteria

'I can turn any paragraph passage into a 50-60 word summary in my own words, using the checklist.'

Differentiation, Exit Ticket & Homework

Support (scaffolding)

  • Give the passage with one example per paragraph already struck out.
  • Provide the connective bank on a card.

Extension

  • Learners reduce the model summary further to 40 words without losing a point.

Exit ticket

Write the first sentence of your summary and count the words aloud.

Homework

Choose a newspaper article of about 200 words and summarise it in 50-60 words. Mark your own using the four self-check questions.
