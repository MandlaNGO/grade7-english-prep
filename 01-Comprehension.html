Module 1: Reading Comprehension

Reading & Literature Skills - understanding and correctly answering comprehension questions.

Grade 7 · English Home Language (HL) · Term 4 exam revision · 2 x 60 minutes

By the end of this module you can:

  • Apply the three stages of the reading process (pre-, during and post-reading) to a text.
  • Answer literal, inferential and evaluative questions correctly.
  • Use skimming and scanning to locate information quickly.
  • Distinguish fact from opinion and infer the meaning of unfamiliar words.
  • Write a summary in paragraph form within a set word limit.

1. Learner Notes

The reading process

StageWhat you do
Pre-readingPredict from the title and pictures; activate what you already know; set a purpose for reading.
During readingRead for main ideas; ask questions; note unfamiliar words; monitor whether you understand.
Post-readingAnswer questions; find evidence in the text; compare and contrast; evaluate; summarise.

Reading strategies

StrategyMeaningWhen to use it
SkimmingRead quickly for the main ideaFirst read - to get the gist
ScanningLook quickly for specific detailsTo find a name, date, number or key word
Intensive readingSlow, careful readingTo answer detailed questions
PredictingGuess what comes nextPre-reading and during reading
InferringWork out meaning that is not stated directly'What does this tell us?'
VisualisingPicture the scene in your mindNarrative texts

Types of comprehension questions

  • Literal - the answer is stated directly in the text (find it with scanning).
  • Inferential - the answer is suggested but not stated; you must read between the lines.
  • Evaluative - you give your own opinion and support it with reasons from the text.
  • Vocabulary in context - work out what a word means from the words around it.
  • Fact and opinion - decide whether a statement can be proved (fact) or is a belief (opinion).

How to answer for marks

  • Read the question twice and underline the command word (name, list, describe, explain, discuss).
  • Check the mark allocation: 2 marks usually need two points or a point plus a reason.
  • Answer in full sentences unless told otherwise.
  • Use your own words unless you are asked to quote.
  • Give evidence: 'The text says ...' or quote a short phrase.
  • Never leave a question blank - a sensible attempt can earn part marks.

Writing a summary

  1. Read the passage and underline the main idea of each paragraph.
  2. Cross out examples, repetition and unimportant detail.
  3. Paraphrase each main idea in your own words.
  4. Join the ideas with linking words (firstly, then, as a result).
  5. Stay within the word limit and write in paragraph form, in the present tense.

3. Worked Example

Passage: The Long Walk

Early each morning, Thabo walked two kilometres to the river before school. The water jars were heavy, but he never complained. His grandmother needed the water to cook and to wash. One cold morning, Thabo found the river frozen at the edges. He broke the thin ice with a stone and filled the jars. On his way home, he slipped and one jar cracked. Thabo sat down and cried. A neighbour passing by smiled kindly, helped him up and shared her own water. That day Thabo learnt that help often comes from unexpected places.

Questions and answers

  1. Why did Thabo walk to the river? (1) - Because his grandmother needed water to cook and wash.
  2. How do we know Thabo was hard-working? (2) - He walked two kilometres early every morning and never complained.
  3. What does the word 'cracked' mean in this text? (1) - Broke or split.
  4. What lesson did Thabo learn? (2) - Help often comes from unexpected places; people are kind.

4. Worksheet

Passage: The Market Day

The school hall was buzzing like a beehive. Tables sagged under cakes, beads and second-hand books. Lerato's stall was the busiest of all. She had spent three weekends baking koeksisters with her aunt. Now her tray was almost empty. 'Sell one more and you can buy the paint set,' her friend whispered. Suddenly a small boy dropped his coins. Without thinking, Lerato helped him pick them up and gave him a free koeksister. She did not sell the last batch she had hoped for, but her smile was the widest in the hall.

Answer the questions in full sentences

  1. Where does the passage take place? (1)
  2. What does the simile 'buzzing like a beehive' tell us about the hall? (2)
  3. How much time did Lerato spend preparing for the market? (1)
  4. Is this statement a fact or an opinion: 'Lerato's stall was the busiest of all.' Give a reason. (2)
  5. Why did Lerato give the boy a free koeksister? (2)
  6. What kind of person is Lerato? Support your answer with evidence from the text. (3)
  7. Write a summary of the passage in 30-40 words. (5)

7. Remember this!

One-glance recap - if a line feels fuzzy, scroll back to the notes.

  • Read the questions first - then you know what to look for.
  • Answer in full sentences, using words from the question.
  • One mark = one point. Add a reason when the question asks why.
  • Quote the exact words from the text as proof (in quotation marks).

Lesson plan, differentiation, exit ticket and homework for this module.

Lesson Plan

Learning objectives

  • Apply the three stages of the reading process (pre-, during and post-reading) to a text.
  • Answer literal, inferential and evaluative questions correctly.
  • Use skimming and scanning to locate information quickly.
  • Distinguish fact from opinion and infer the meaning of unfamiliar words.
  • Write a summary in paragraph form within a set word limit.

Prior knowledge

Learners can read aloud, retell a story and identify a main idea.

Resources

Comprehension passages (see Worksheet), dictionary, whiteboard, timer, learner workbooks.

Teaching steps

TimeActivity
10 minActivate prior knowledge: discuss how we read for meaning and what makes a good answer.
15 minModel the reading process on a short passage (teacher think-aloud): predict, skim, scan, answer.
15 minGuided practice: class answers literal and inferential questions together; teacher questions the reasoning.
15 minTeach the summary method; class drafts one summary together.
5 minExit ticket: one inferential question answered individually.

Differentiation, Exit Ticket & Homework

Support (scaffolding)

  • Provide the questions before reading and a glossary of hard words.
  • Let learners highlight the answer in the text before writing it.

Extension

  • Ask learners to write two evaluative questions of their own, with model answers.

Exit ticket

Write one inferential question about the passage and answer it in one sentence.

Homework

Find a short newspaper article, answer four questions (one of each type) and write a 40-word summary.
