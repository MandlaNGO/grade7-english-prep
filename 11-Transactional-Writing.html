Module 11: Transactional Writing

Writing & Presenting - formal and informal everyday texts with the right format and register.

Grade 7 · English Home Language (HL) · Term 4 exam revision · 2 x 60 minutes

By the end of this module you can:

  • Match the purpose, audience and register of a transactional text.
  • Use the correct format for letters, emails, diary entries, reviews, instructions, adverts, notices and minutes.
  • Write 2 short or 1 long transactional text under time.
  • Proofread with the C.O.P.S. checklist.

1. Learner Notes

Purpose, audience, register

Ask yourselfExamples
Who am I writing to?Principal (formal) / my friend (informal) / readers of the school newsletter
What do I want to happen?Persuade, apologise, invite, inform, complain, thank
Formal or informal?Formal: staff, businesses, officials. Informal: diary, close friends and family

Format cards - the must-haves

Text typeMust-haves
Formal letterYour address, date, recipient's title/address, 'Dear Sir/Madam' (or Dear Mr/Ms + surname), clear paragraphs, 'Yours faithfully' (Sir/Madam) or 'Yours sincerely' (name), your signature and name
EmailTo:, Subject: (short and clear), greeting, short paragraphs, sign-off (Kind regards / Yours sincerely), your name
Diary entryDate, 'Dear Diary', first person, feelings and personal tone, sign-off by first name
Book review / letter to authorTitle and author, what it is about (no spoilers), your opinion with reasons, who should read it / a rating
InstructionsTitle, materials/ingredients list, numbered steps in order, imperative verbs (mix, cut, pour), warnings (careful!)
Advertisement / posterBold headline or slogan, descriptive + persuasive words, price/contact details, a call to action, visuals
NoticeThe word NOTICE or a clear heading, date, time, place, who it concerns, contact person, signature
Agenda and minutesAgenda: date/venue, apologies, numbered items with times. Minutes: items discussed, decisions, action points and who is responsible

Friendly letter / message (informal)

Greeting (Hi Jason), chatty body in paragraphs, feeling words, sign-off (Cheers / Love / Your friend) + first name.

Formal vs informal phrases

FormalInformal
I am writing to request...Can I get...?
I would appreciate your reply.Write back soon!
Yours faithfullyCheers
Kind regardsLove

C.O.P.S. proofreading

  • Capitals - names, days, start of sentences, 'Dear'.
  • Order - do the parts follow the format? Right order of steps?
  • Punctuation - full stops, commas in lists, apostrophes.
  • Spelling - check the words you often miss (there/their, your/you're).

2. Interactive Practice

3. Worked Example

Model email (annotated)

[To:] mrndlovu@sunnyviewprimary.edu.za

[Subject:] Request for permission to hold a recycling drive

Dear Mr Ndlovu

[Paragraph 1 - who I am and why I write] I am the secretary of the Grade 7 Recycling Club. I am writing to ask for permission to hold a recycling drive at school next Friday.

[Paragraph 2 - details] With your approval, members will collect paper, tins and plastic bottles during second break. We will store everything in the hall until the collection company arrives at 14:00.

[Paragraph 3 - polite close] I would appreciate your reply before Wednesday so that we can prepare posters. Thank you for considering our request.

Kind regards

Sam Mokoena (Grade 7B)

Why it works

  • Subject line is short and clear.
  • Three short paragraphs: reason, details, polite close.
  • Formal register [in plain words: the level of language you choose - formal or informal]: 'I am writing to ask', 'I would appreciate'.
  • Correct sign-off and full name.

MEMO - for checking your work, not for copying

Task A - Notice checklist

  • Heading/NOTICE with the event name.
  • Date, time and place (Friday, second break, school hall).
  • Who it concerns (all learners).
  • What to bring / what happens.
  • Contact person and signature.

Task A - Diary entry checklist

  • Date + 'Dear Diary'.
  • First person, informal, personal tone.
  • Feelings before and after the event (nervous, proud).
  • Sign-off with first name.

Task B - Formal letter model skeleton

Your address; date; The Principal, Sunnyview Primary; Dear Mr/Ms [surname]; Para 1: who I am + reason (broken taps in the bathrooms); Para 2: the problem in detail (water wasting, no washing hands, learners slipping); Para 3: request action politely (please arrange a plumber this week); Yours faithfully/sincerely; signature; full name.

Task B - Book review skeleton

Title and author; what the book is about (main character and problem, no ending spoiler); what I liked and why (with one example); one small criticism; rating out of 5 and who should read it.

Marking guide

  • Format: 5 marks (all must-haves present and in the right order).
  • Content: 5 marks (clear purpose, complete details).
  • Register/language: 5 marks (right tone, correct sentences).
  • Spelling and punctuation: 5 marks.
  • Total per long text: 20 (short tasks: use half of each category).

6. Exam-style Question & Memo

Paper 3 style - Transactional writing (2 short or 1 long)

Prompts: (a) Write an email to your favourite author telling them how their book changed your reading life. (b) Write instructions for preparing a healthy lunchbox. (c) Write a notice about lost property at school. (d) Write a formal letter to the municipality about litter in the park.

Time: about 30 minutes for 2 short texts, or 45 minutes for 1 long text. Proofread with C.O.P.S.

Show memo (marking notes) (click to reveal)
  • (a) Email: To/Subject, greeting, praise + one example from the book, polite sign-off. Register: friendly but respectful.
  • (b) Instructions: title, materials, numbered imperative steps in order, one warning. Register: clear and direct.
  • (c) Notice: heading, what was lost, where and when, where to collect, contact, signature.
  • (d) Formal letter: addresses, date, 'Dear Sir/Madam', problem + impact + request, 'Yours faithfully', signature.
  • Award format marks first: a beautiful text in the wrong format loses marks before content is even read.

7. Remember this!

One-glance recap - if a line feels fuzzy, scroll back to the notes.

  • Format first - a wrong format loses marks before your content is even read.
  • Purpose and audience decide the register: formal for officials, friendly for friends.
  • Formal = no slang and full sentences. Friendly = warm but still polite.
  • Marks are shared: format, content and language all count.

Lesson plan, differentiation, exit ticket and homework for this module.

Lesson Plan

Learning objectives

  • Match the purpose, audience and register of a transactional text.
  • Use the correct format for letters, emails, diary entries, reviews, instructions, adverts, notices and minutes.
  • Write 2 short or 1 long transactional text under time.
  • Proofread with the C.O.P.S. checklist.

Prior knowledge

Learners have written friendly letters and messages; they know the difference between formal and informal tone.

Resources

Format cards (notes), model email, worksheet tasks and memo/rubric.

Lesson flow (2 x 60 minutes)

  1. Session 1: Purpose, audience, register + format cards; class 'spot the format error'.
  2. Session 1: Model email annotated; formal vs informal phrase sorting.
  3. Session 2: Worksheet - Task A (2 short) or Task B (1 long) under time (30 minutes).
  4. Session 2: Peer marking with the rubric; debrief on top three errors.

Success criteria

'I can name the must-haves of each format, choose the right register, and write the task in the time given.'

Differentiation, Exit Ticket & Homework

Support (scaffolding)

  • Provide printed format skeleton frames with blanks.
  • Give the formal/informal phrase table on a card.

Extension

  • Learners rewrite an informal email as a formal letter and highlight every register change.

Exit ticket

Name the must-haves of a notice (heading, date, time, place, who, contact) from memory.

Homework

Write the transactional text you did NOT attempt in class. Swap with a partner and mark format only using the checklist.
