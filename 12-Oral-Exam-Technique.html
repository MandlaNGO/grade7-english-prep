Module 12: Oral & Exam Technique

Preparing & Presenting - the oral presentation, exam pacing and proofreading.

Grade 7 · English Home Language (HL) · Term 4 exam revision · 2 x 60 minutes

By the end of this module you can:

  • Plan and present the Creative Writing Project oral presentation.
  • Use pace, volume, eye contact and expression effectively.
  • Name the three exam papers, their marks and question structure.
  • Plan pacing for Paper 2 and apply the 'marks = points' rule.
  • Use the C.O.P.S. proofreading checklist and an exam-day routine.

1. Learner Notes

Paper 1 - the oral presentation (20 marks)

PartWhat to do
StructureGreeting; introduction (topic + why it matters); 3-5 main points; conclusion; thank you
DeliveryPace (not too fast); volume (back row must hear); eye contact; posture (stand tall); expression (let your voice show feeling)
Visual aidOne clear poster or object; point to it only when you mention it
RehearsalPractise 3 times: alone, to one person, in front of a mirror or recording
TimeKeep to the time given; finish your conclusion even if you are nervous

Paper 2 - Response to Texts (60 marks)

QuestionWhat it testsMarks
Q1Literary / non-literary text (comprehension)20
Q2Visual text10
Q3Summary10
Q4Language structures and conventions20

Suggested pacing for a 90-minute paper: Q1 30 min - Q2 12 min - Q3 15 min - Q4 25 min - checking 8 min. Check your own exam's time and divide by marks.

The golden rule: marks = points

  • 1 mark = 1 point; 2 marks = 2 points or a point + reason; 3 marks = 3 points or a point + evidence + explanation.
  • Never leave a blank - an attempt can earn part marks.
  • Read the question verb: name (one word), describe (say what it is like), explain (say why/how), discuss (points for and against), evaluate (judgement + reasons).

Q4 language survival checklist

  • Nouns: common, proper, abstract, collective, concrete; plurals.
  • Pronouns: personal, possessive, reflexive, demonstrative, interrogative, relative.
  • Articles: a / an / the.
  • Adjectives: comparative (-er / more) and superlative (-est / most).
  • Prefixes and suffixes (im-, un-, -ful, -less); synonyms and antonyms.
  • Sentence types: simple, compound, complex.
  • Tense consistency; past/present/future.
  • Direct and indirect speech.
  • Active and passive voice [in plain words: the doer is the subject (The chef cooked the meal) or the object becomes the subject (The meal was cooked by the chef)].
  • Punctuation: full stop, comma, question mark, exclamation mark, apostrophe, quotation marks.
  • Figures of speech: simile, metaphor, personification, alliteration, hyperbole, idiom.
  • Subject-verb agreement (the dog barks / the dogs bark).

Paper 3 - writing

  • Choose 2 short or 1 long text - decide quickly and commit.
  • Follow the format exactly (see Module 11).
  • Plan in 10 minutes; write; leave 5 minutes for C.O.P.S.

C.O.P.S. proofreading

LetterCheck
CCapitals: names, days, months, sentence starts, 'I'
OOrder: format correct? Steps in order? Paragraphs in order?
PPunctuation: full stops, commas, apostrophes, question marks, quotes
SSpelling: your personal trouble words (there/their, your/you're, because)

Exam-day routine

  • Sleep early; eat breakfast; arrive early with pen, pencil, ruler and eraser.
  • Read the instructions and all questions before writing.
  • Number your answers exactly as the paper does.
  • Check the clock halfway; adjust your pace.
  • Cross out neatly; do not scribble over words.

2. Interactive Practice

3. Worked Example

Model oral opening and closing

Opening:

Good morning, class and Ms Naidoo. Imagine finding a map that leads to a place no one has ever shown you. That is what writing my own story felt like. Today I will share why my Creative Writing Project changed the way I read.

Closing:

Writing taught me that every reader is also a writer in waiting. Thank you for listening - I hope you find your own map soon.

Why it works

  • It builds a picture ('imagine...') to hook the audience.
  • It states the topic and the journey in one sentence.
  • The closing ends with a message, not 'that's all'.
  • Both are short enough to memorise and say naturally.
  • Delivery notes: pause after 'Imagine...'; slow down on the message sentence; look at three points in the room.

7. Remember this!

One-glance recap - if a line feels fuzzy, scroll back to the notes.

  • Oral: know your opening and closing by heart, rehearse aloud 3 times, speak to the back row.
  • In the exam: read all the questions first, share out your time, check the clock at halfway.
  • Never leave a blank - an attempt can earn part marks.
  • Proofread with C.O.P.S.: Capitals, Order, Punctuation, Spelling.

Lesson plan, differentiation, exit ticket and homework for this module.

Lesson Plan

Learning objectives

  • Plan and present the Creative Writing Project oral presentation.
  • Use pace, volume, eye contact and expression effectively.
  • Name the three exam papers, their marks and question structure.
  • Plan pacing for Paper 2 and apply the 'marks = points' rule.
  • Use the C.O.P.S. proofreading checklist and an exam-day routine.

Prior knowledge

Learners have prepared their Creative Writing Project; they have written class presentations before.

Resources

Oral checklist, pacing table, exam map table, stopwatch (phone or classroom clock).

Lesson flow (2 x 60 minutes)

  1. Session 1: Paper 1 - oral structure and delivery skills; rehearse in pairs with the checklist.
  2. Session 1: Q4 language survival checklist - quick-fire review.
  3. Session 2: Exam map + pacing for Paper 2; C.O.P.S. proofreading drills on real error examples.
  4. Session 2: Mock run - 'The final week' planning task and exam-day checklist.

Success criteria

'I can deliver my oral with a clear opening and closing, and I know exactly how to spend my time in every paper.'

Differentiation, Exit Ticket & Homework

Support (scaffolding)

  • Allow the oral outline to be written in bullet form and practised with a partner first.

Extension

  • Learners run a class 'exam clinic': each presents one Q4 language rule with an example and a practice item.

Exit ticket

Say your oral hook out loud to a partner; the partner gives one star and one wish.

Homework

Rehearse the oral three times (alone, to someone, recorded) and complete the personal exam-day checklist.
