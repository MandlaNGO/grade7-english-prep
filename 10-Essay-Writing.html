Module 10: Essay Writing

Writing & Presenting - narrative, descriptive and reflective essays.

Grade 7 · English Home Language (HL) · Term 4 exam revision · 2 x 60 minutes

By the end of this module you can:

  • Tell the difference between narrative, descriptive and reflective essays.
  • Use the full writing process: plan, draft, revise, edit, proofread.
  • Plan an essay with a planning grid.
  • Write TEE paragraphs with a hook introduction.
  • Self-check an essay against the marking criteria.

1. Learner Notes

Three essay types

TypeWhat it doesSigns
NarrativeTells a story with eventsPast tense, characters, a problem, a climax, an ending
DescriptivePaints a picture of a place, person or momentSensory words: sights, sounds, smells, feelings
ReflectiveShares an experience AND what you learnt from it'I learnt...', 'Looking back...', thoughts and feelings

The writing process

  1. Plan - use the planning grid (10 minutes).
  2. Draft - write your ideas without stopping to perfect them.
  3. Revise - improve content and order; add sensory detail.
  4. Edit - fix grammar, tense and sentence structure.
  5. Proofread - check spelling and punctuation with fresh eyes.

Paragraph power - TEE

LetterPartExample
TTopic sentence - what the paragraph is aboutThe storm hit our street at midnight.
EEvidence - detail or example that proves itWind tore at the roof and water poured under the door.
EExplanation - what it shows or why it mattersWe realised how quickly life can change.

Hooks and closers

  • Hooks: a question ('Have you ever felt invisible?'), a sound ('Crash!'), an action ('The bus was already moving when...'), dialogue ('"Run!" she shouted.').
  • Closers: the lesson learnt, echoing the opening, or a final feeling.

Show, don't tell

TellShow
She was nervous.Her hands trembled as she opened the envelope.
It was cold.Her breath smoked in the morning air.
He was happy.He could not stop the smile spreading across his face.

What markers look for

CriterionWhat it means
Content and planningInteresting, complete ideas that fit the topic
StructureHook, ordered paragraphs, strong ending
Language and styleVaried sentences, sensory words, correct tense
Spelling and punctuationCorrect spelling; capital letters, commas, quotes

2. Interactive Practice

3. Worked Example

Prompt: Write about a day you will never forget.

Model introduction (hook + direction)

The bus was already pulling away when I saw my grandmother's red scarf slip from my hand. That was the moment the longest day of my life began - the day I learnt that being lost can help you find what matters most.

Model body paragraph (TEE labelled)

[T] The streets of the city turned into a maze I could not read. [E] Every corner looked the same; taxis hooted past and the sun burned the back of my neck while my mielie-meal money grew damp in my fist. [E] I realised that being brave is not about being unafraid - it is about taking the next step even when your heart is racing.

Why these are strong

  • The hook starts in the middle of action - you want to read on.
  • The introduction promises a lesson, not just events.
  • The body paragraph shows (maze, hooting taxis, damp money) instead of telling.
  • The paragraph ends with a reflection that fits the story.

4. Worksheet

Task (30 minutes)

  1. Choose ONE prompt: (a) Write about a day you will never forget. (b) Describe a place that makes you feel safe. (c) Write about a time you learnt something the hard way.
  2. Complete the planning grid for your prompt.
  3. Write the introduction and ONE body paragraph (labelled TEE in the margin).

Self-check

  • Is my hook one of: question, sound, action or dialogue?
  • Does my body paragraph follow TEE?
  • Did I use at least two sensory details?
  • Is my tense consistent (past for narrative)?
  • Did I proofread for capitals and full stops?

Marking guide

  • Content and planning - is the story complete and does it fit the topic?
  • Structure - hook, ordered paragraphs, ending.
  • Language and style - sensory language, varied sentences.
  • Spelling and punctuation.
  • Reward risk-taking and strong verbs; correct recurring errors in the margin for the learner's error log.

6. Exam-style Question & Memo

Paper 3 style - Essay (write before the exam session)

Write an essay of 150-200 words (check your teacher's exact requirement) on ONE of these prompts: (1) Write about a moment when you were proud of yourself. (2) Describe the sights and sounds of a busy Saturday morning in your neighbourhood. (3) Write about a time a friend surprised you.

Allow 45-60 minutes: plan 10, draft 25, revise and proofread 10-15.

Show memo (marking notes) (click to reveal)
  • Prompt 1 (reflective): expect an experience + what was learnt; look for thoughts and feelings, not only events.
  • Prompt 2 (descriptive): expect sensory detail (sights, sounds, smells) and organised movement through the scene - not a story.
  • Prompt 3 (narrative): expect characters, a problem, a climax and an ending; past tense consistent.
  • Use the four criteria table: content and planning, structure, language and style, spelling and punctuation.
  • Common deductions: wrong genre, tense slips, no paragraphing, weak ending, copied class examples.

7. Remember this!

One-glance recap - if a line feels fuzzy, scroll back to the notes.

  • Plan first: hook, body paragraphs, closer.
  • TEE a paragraph: Topic sentence, Evidence or example, Explanation.
  • Show, don't tell - describe actions and senses instead of naming the feeling.
  • Keep 5 minutes at the end to proofread with C.O.P.S.

Lesson plan, differentiation, exit ticket and homework for this module.

Lesson Plan

Learning objectives

  • Tell the difference between narrative, descriptive and reflective essays.
  • Use the full writing process: plan, draft, revise, edit, proofread.
  • Plan an essay with a planning grid.
  • Write TEE paragraphs with a hook introduction.
  • Self-check an essay against the marking criteria.

Prior knowledge

Learners can write a paragraph with a topic sentence and use the past tense.

Resources

Learner notes, planning grid, model introduction and paragraph, essay prompts.

Lesson flow (2 x 60 minutes)

  1. Session 1: The three essay types + the writing process; hook and closer bank.
  2. Session 1: TEE paragraphs modelled; 'show, don't tell' practice.
  3. Session 2: Planning grid for a chosen prompt, then write the introduction + one body paragraph (30 minutes).
  4. Session 2: Peer feedback with the rubric, then a teacher-led debrief.

Success criteria

'I can plan an essay in 10 minutes, write a hook introduction and a labelled TEE paragraph, and name what markers look for.'

Differentiation, Exit Ticket & Homework

Support (scaffolding)

  • Provide a sentence-starter bank for hooks and TEE paragraphs.
  • Allow oral planning before writing.

Extension

  • Learners write the full essay at home using the same grid and self-mark with the rubric before submitting.

Exit ticket

Write one hook sentence for prompt (c) and underline the sensory detail you would use.

Homework

Complete the essay from the worksheet prompt. Highlight your hook and one TEE paragraph before you hand it in.
