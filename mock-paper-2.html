Question 1: Literary Text 20 marks

Read the passage below and answer the questions that follow.

Naledi had never been so nervous in her life. It was the day of the district athletics finals, and the whole school had come to watch. Sunnyview Primary had never won the relay, but this year the team believed they had a chance. Naledi was chosen to run the last leg - the most important one.

The starting whistle blew. Thandi flew down the track like a swallow and handed the baton to Sipho, who powered through the second leg. Then it was Naledi's turn. She stretched out her hand, but as the baton touched her fingers, her palms - slippery as wet soap - let it slip. The baton bounced twice on the track and rolled into the grass.

For one terrible second, the crowd went silent. Naledi wanted to run away and hide, but she saw her team waiting at the finish line, arms open. She grabbed the baton, chased the three runners ahead of her and gave everything she had.

She did not catch them. Sunnyview finished fourth. Afterwards, Naledi sat alone on the grass, tears on her cheeks. Coach Dlamini walked over and sat down next to her. 'We lost because of me,' she whispered.

The coach shook his head. 'Did you see your teammates? Not one of them blamed you. A team that runs together in bad luck is stronger than one that only celebrates in good luck.' Naledi looked up. Her teammates were walking towards her, carrying water and shouting her name. She was not a winner today - not on the scoreboard - but as she stood up and walked to meet them, she felt like something much more valuable had been won.

Questions

  1. What event was taking place, and what had Sunnyview Primary never done? (2)
  2. Why were Naledi's palms 'slippery as wet soap'? (2)
  3. Quote the simile that describes how Thandi ran. (2)
  4. What does 'For one terrible second, the crowd went silent' tell us about the moment? (2)
  5. Is this a fact or an opinion? Give a reason: 'Sunnyview Primary had never won the relay.' (2)
  6. Describe TWO qualities of Naledi and give evidence from the passage for each. (4)
  7. Why did Coach Dlamini shake his head when Naledi blamed herself? (2)
  8. What is the message of this passage? (2)
  9. Find a word in paragraph 5 that means 'said someone was responsible for something bad'. Use it in your own sentence. (2)

Q1 MEMO - 20 marks
  1. 1. The district athletics finals; Sunnyview had never won the relay. (1 + 1)
  2. 2. She was extremely nervous and her hands were sweating. Award for the reason + link to nervousness. (2)
  3. 3. 'Thandi flew down the track like a swallow.' (2)
  4. 4. Everyone was shocked and frozen - they could not believe she had dropped the baton; it shows how serious the mistake felt. (2)
  5. 5. Fact - it can be checked in school records / it can be proved. (2)
  6. 6. Any two: determined / never gave up - she chased the runners and 'gave everything she had'; honest - she admitted 'We lost because of me'; brave - she did not run away and hide; team-spirited - she stood up and went to meet her teammates. (2 + 2)
  7. 7. He disagreed with her - he wanted her to see that the team supported her and that no one blamed her. (2)
  8. 8. Winning is not everything - teamwork, support and standing together in bad luck matter more. Accept any well-worded version. (2)
  9. 9. 'blamed' (1) + any correct original sentence using 'blamed' (1).

Question 3: Summary 10 marks

Read the passage and summarise it in your own words in 50-60 words, in ONE paragraph.

Honey bees are among the busiest and most useful insects on Earth. Every day, thousands of worker bees leave the hive to collect nectar and pollen from flowers. As they travel from plant to plant, they carry pollen on their bodies, which helps flowers and crops produce fruit and seeds. In fact, about one third of the food we eat depends on the work of bees. Bees also make honey, which people have used as food and medicine for thousands of years. Inside the hive every bee has a job: the queen lays eggs, workers clean, guard and collect food, and drones help the colony reproduce. When a worker finds a rich patch of flowers, it performs a special 'waggle dance' to tell the others exactly where to go. Sadly, bee numbers are dropping. Pesticides, diseases and the loss of wild flowers make it hard for bees to survive. Planting flowers, avoiding harmful chemicals and protecting trees can help these small heroes. Without bees, our plates - and our economy - would look very different.

Write your summary in 50-60 words. (10)

Main points (any 7)

  • Bees are busy, useful insects; workers collect nectar and pollen.
  • Pollen helps flowers and crops produce fruit and seeds.
  • About one third of our food depends on bees.
  • Bees make honey used as food and medicine.

Model summary

Bees are busy, useful insects whose pollen helps plants produce fruit and seeds, and about one third of our food depends on them. They also make honey used as food and medicine. Bees share hive jobs and use a waggle dance to guide others to flowers. Sadly, pesticides and habitat loss are reducing their numbers, so we should plant flowers and protect trees.

Marking: Content: any 7 points = 7 marks. Own words = 1. Within 50-60 words = 1. Paragraph flow and connectives = 1. Total 10.

Question 4: Language Structures 20 marks

Answer all the questions. Read each question carefully.

  1. Identify the abstract noun: 'The team showed great courage in the final.' (2)
  2. Identify the possessive pronoun: 'We gave them our water.' (2)
  3. Fill in the correct article: 'Naledi grabbed ___ baton from the grass.' (2)
  4. Complete with the correct comparative: 'This race was bad; yesterday's was even ___.' (2)
  5. Give the opposite of 'fair' using a prefix. (2)
  6. Give a synonym of 'brave' and an antonym of 'victory'. (2)
  7. Rewrite with correct punctuation: where is my baton (2)
  8. Change to the past tense: 'The team runs fast.' (2)
  9. Rewrite in indirect speech: Naledi said, 'I am sorry.' (2)
  10. Rewrite in the passive voice: 'The coach praised the team.' (2)
