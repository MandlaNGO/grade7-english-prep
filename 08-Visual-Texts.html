Module 8: Visual Texts

Reading & Viewing - cartoons, adverts, posters and graphs.

Grade 7 · English Home Language (HL) · Term 4 exam revision · 2 x 60 minutes

By the end of this module you can:

  • Identify the type, audience and purpose of a visual text.
  • Decode speech bubbles, body language, facial expressions, colour and font.
  • Explain the persuasive techniques used in adverts and posters.
  • Explain a cartoonist's intention.
  • Read simple graphs (bar, line, pie) for information.

1. Learner Notes

Step 1 - Type, audience, purpose

Visual typeWhere you find itWhat it does
CartoonNewspapers, magazinesMakes a point about people, events or society - often funny or critical
AdvertTV, posters, social mediaPersuades you to buy or do something
PosterSchools, clinics, streetsInforms or reminds a community
GraphReports, textbooks, newsShows information as numbers and trends
InfographicWebsites, magazinesExplains information quickly with pictures and facts

Step 2 - Decode the picture

  • Speech bubbles and thought bubbles - what is said, and what is NOT said?
  • Facial expressions - happy, worried, angry, surprised?
  • Body language - pointing, crossed arms, shrug, thumbs up.
  • Colour - red = danger or urgency; green = nature, safe; blue = calm, water.
  • Size and position - the biggest thing matters most.
  • Font - big bold letters shout; handwriting feels personal.
  • Symbols - a heart = love; a rand sign = money; a clock = time.

Step 3 - Persuasive techniques

TechniqueWhat it looks like
SloganA short, memorable phrase: 'Every drop counts!'
Emotive wordsWords that push feelings: cruel, miracle, deadly, cosy
RepetitionThe same word or sound again and again
Questions & exclamations'Are you still wasting water?' / 'Act now!'
Statistics'Nine out of ten learners recycle.'
Witnesses or celebritiesA famous face telling you it works
Logo & brand coloursThe same symbol and colours everywhere
Call to actionAn instruction: 'Join today!' 'Buy now!'

Graphs - read before you answer

  • Title - what the graph is about.
  • Axes - what is measured on each side (labels!).
  • Key or legend - what each colour or bar means.
  • Trend words: increase, decrease, stay the same, highest, lowest, peak.

Answer frame

The purpose of this visual is to ... The target audience is ... The artist shows this by using ... (technique) and ... (evidence from the picture).

2. Interactive Practice

3. Worked Example

Model: answering a poster question

Q: What is the purpose of a clinic poster that says 'Test early, live healthy'? (2)

A: To persuade people in the community to test for illness early. It uses an emotive promise ('live healthy') and a command (call to action). (2)

Q: How does the artist make the message urgent? (2)

A: Strong colours and words like 'early' and 'live' create urgency: early testing is linked to staying alive. (2)

What strong answers always do

  • Name the technique (slogan / emotive words / call to action).
  • Point to evidence in the visual ('the smiling water drop').
  • Explain the effect on the reader ('it makes saving water feel easy and friendly').

7. Remember this!

One-glance recap - if a line feels fuzzy, scroll back to the notes.

  • Start with three questions: What type is it? Who is it for? What must I do after seeing it?
  • Bigger, more central and brighter = more important. Colours and fonts carry feelings.
  • Spot the persuasion tools: emotive words, slogans, calls to action.
  • Graphs: read the title, labels and units first - then describe the trend.

Lesson plan, differentiation, exit ticket and homework for this module.

Lesson Plan

Learning objectives

  • Identify the type, audience and purpose of a visual text.
  • Decode speech bubbles, body language, facial expressions, colour and font.
  • Explain the persuasive techniques used in adverts and posters.
  • Explain a cartoonist's intention.
  • Read simple graphs (bar, line, pie) for information.

Prior knowledge

Learners have seen comic strips, posters and adverts; they can describe what a picture shows.

Resources

Learner notes, practice visuals and memos; colour pencils for extension work.

Lesson flow (2 x 60 minutes)

  1. Session 1: Type, audience, purpose + decoding toolkit, using the water poster.
  2. Session 1: Persuasive techniques - class spots them in real adverts.
  3. Session 2: Graphs - parts of a graph and trend words.
  4. Session 2: Worksheet visual (poster + graph), then the exam-style cartoon under time (12 minutes).

Success criteria

'I can say what a visual is for, who it targets, and explain two techniques the artist uses to persuade.'

Differentiation, Exit Ticket & Homework

Support (scaffolding)

  • Provide the answer frame and a list of question starters.
  • Pair learners to talk through the cartoon before writing.

Extension

  • Learners design their own poster with one slogan, one emotive word and one call to action.

Exit ticket

In one sentence each: name one technique in the recycling cartoon and its effect on you.

Homework

Find any advert at home or in a newspaper. Identify type, audience, purpose and two techniques. Bring it to class.
