Module 6: Sentences, Punctuation & Verbs

Language & Grammar - punctuation; finite and non-finite verbs; simple, compound and complex sentences; adjectival and adverbial clauses; direct and indirect speech.

Grade 7 · English Home Language (HL) · Term 4 exam revision · 2-3 x 60 minutes

By the end of this module you can:

  • Use punctuation marks correctly.
  • Distinguish finite from non-finite verbs.
  • Identify and write simple, compound and complex sentences.
  • Identify adjectival (relative) and adverbial clauses.
  • Change direct speech into indirect (reported) speech.

1. Learner Notes

Punctuation

MarkUseExample
Full stop .Ends a statementHe left early.
Comma ,Separates items, clauses or adds a pauseI bought apples, pears and plums.
Semi-colon ;Joins two related clausesIt rained; the match was cancelled.
Colon :Introduces a list or explanationBring these: a pen, a ruler and a book.
Apostrophe 'Shows possession or a contractionthe girl's bag; don't
Quotation marks " "Show a speaker's exact words"Come in," she said.
Exclamation mark !Shows strong feelingWhat a day!
Question mark ?Ends a questionAre you ready?
Ellipsis ...Shows an omitted part or trailing offI was going to say ...
Hyphen -Joins wordswell-known author

Rules to remember: capitalise the first word of a sentence, proper nouns, 'I' and the names of days and months. Every sentence needs a capital letter and an end punctuation mark.

Finite and non-finite verbs

TypeMeaningExample
Finite verb [in plain words: a verb that changes to show the time - I walk, I walked]Shows tense and agrees with the subjectShe runs. They ran.
Non-finite verb [in plain words: a verb form such as to run, running or broken - it cannot be the sentence's main verb]Does not show tense; cannot be the main verb of a sentenceto run (infinitive [in plain words: to + verb - to run, to swim]), running (gerund [in plain words: an -ing verb form doing the job of a noun - Swimming is fun]), broken (participle [in plain words: a verb form used like an adjective - a broken window])

Sentence types

TypeStructureExample
SimpleOne main clauseThe dog barked.
CompoundTwo main clauses joined by and, but, or, so, yet, for, norThe dog barked, and the cat ran.
ComplexOne main clause + one or more subordinate clauses [in plain words: parts of a sentence that cannot stand alone - because a stranger approached]The dog barked because a stranger approached.

Clauses

  • Main (independent) clause - can stand alone as a sentence.
  • Subordinate clause - cannot stand alone; it adds information.
ClauseFunctionExample
Adjectival (relative) [in plain words: a group of words that describes a noun - starts with who, which or that]Describes a noun; starts with who, which, thatThe boy who won the race is my cousin.
Adverbial [in plain words: tells when, where, why or how something happens]Tells when, where, why, how, or under what condition; starts with because, when, if, although, so thatWe left early because the bus was full.

Direct and indirect (reported) speech

ChangeExample
Quotation marks removed"I am tired," she said. - She said that she was tired.
Tense moves back one stepam - was; will - would; is - was
Pronouns changeI - he/she; we - they
Time/place words changetoday - that day; here - there; tomorrow - the next day

Introductory verbs: said that, told (someone) that, asked, replied, explained.

7. Remember this!

One-glance recap - if a line feels fuzzy, scroll back to the notes.

  • Simple = 1 part. Compound = 2 parts joined by and/but/or. Complex = a main part + a part that cannot stand alone.
  • Finite verb changes with the time (I walk, I walked); non-finite does not (to run, running, broken).
  • Adjectival clause describes a noun (who, which, that); adverbial clause tells when, where or how.
  • Direct speech uses quotation marks. Indirect speech usually shifts the tense one step back.

Lesson plan, differentiation, exit ticket and homework for this module.

Lesson Plan

Learning objectives

  • Use punctuation marks correctly.
  • Distinguish finite from non-finite verbs.
  • Identify and write simple, compound and complex sentences.
  • Identify adjectival (relative) and adverbial clauses.
  • Change direct speech into indirect (reported) speech.

Prior knowledge

Learners can identify the subject and verb of a sentence.

Resources

Punctuation chart, sentence-type cards, worksheet, whiteboard.

Teaching steps

TimeActivity
10 minPunctuation revision and quick-fix drill.
10 minFinite vs non-finite verbs; guided identification.
15 minSimple, compound and complex sentences with conjunctions.
15 minAdjectival and adverbial clauses; identify in sentences.
15 minDirect and indirect speech; transform sentences.
5 minExit ticket.

Differentiation, Exit Ticket & Homework

Support

  • Give sentence-type cards and a conjunction word bank; do clause work orally first.

Extension

  • Write a short dialogue and then rewrite it as reported speech.

Exit ticket

Change this to indirect speech: 'I am hungry,' said the boy.

Homework

Write 6 sentences: 2 simple, 2 compound, 2 complex. Underline the clauses.
